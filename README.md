A quantitative formulation of the Farid Quantum Command Unit (FQCU) and Tanfarid
Quantum Thermodynamic Universe (TQTU) programmes requires precise definitions of
energy, entropy, information, and experimental testability. This article develops those
definitions within a specified three-dimensional rotor model while distinguishing
mathematical consistency, numerical verification, physical matching, and discrimination
among competing models. A local Hamiltonian determines a site energy and an antisymmetric
nearest-neighbour energy current. For a separately defined, undriven Langevin process
coupled to one thermal bath, the Kramers–Fokker–Planck generator yields non-increasing
relative entropy with respect to Gibbs equilibrium and an exact nonequilibrium free-energy
identity. Mutual information, total correlation, phase order, and susceptibility are defined
separately. A compact quantum rotor extension identifies harmonic reference quantities and
unresolved microscopic freedom. Composite-Higgs benchmarks and lattice dispersion are
examined as conditional matching pathways. A coefficient-matching exercise translates a
published gamma-ray timing bound into a conditional lattice-scale constraint; it is not a new
astronomical data analysis. Finally, a five-gate protocol specifies prediction provenance,
calibration, uncertainty, identifiability, and decision procedures. The construction provides a
mathematical foundation and a research strategy, not experimental confirmation of an FQCU
substrate.

The first three questions can be answered inside a declared mathematical model. The fourth
requires a physical map and a prediction that remains distinguishable after conventional
explanations and uncertainties are considered. This article therefore treats the rotor kernel as a
model investigated within the FQCU programme, not as an already identified constituent of
nature. Its variables are not assumed to be electrons, quarks, photons, or microscopic elements
of spacetime.
Mathematical consistency means that equations and derived results are coherent. Numerical
verification means that an implementation solves or samples the equations with assessed
errors. Physical matching means that a measured system is described within a declared
regime. Experimental discrimination means that independent data favour a specified
construction over relevant alternatives. None of these achievements automatically establishes
the next. A successful rotor analogue would not, by itself, discover a fundamental substrate.
The thermodynamic identities used below are conventional consequences of specified
dynamics [1]. Their value here is to replace ambiguous terminology with a testable model and
to identify the missing physical connections. No uniquely identifying FQCU signature is
claimed.
Consider a periodic simple cubic lattice with N = L³ sites and spacing ℓ. Each site carries an
angular coordinate and a conjugate momentum. Every undirected nearest-neighbour bond is
counted once. We take L large enough that the ordinary six-neighbour cubic-lattice convention
is unambiguous.
The parameters I, J, and ℓ are an effective inertia, an interaction-energy scale, and a prescribed
geometric spacing. The momentum has angular-momentum units and the natural time unit is
the square root of I/J. Identifying this model energy with particle rest energy, gluon energy, or
gravitational vacuum energy requires an additional derivation.
Time independence gives conservation of total energy in the isolated model. Invariance under
a uniform phase shift gives a separate conserved quantity: total conjugate momentum. This
phase symmetry must not be confused with spatial translation symmetry.

If the proposed effect can be reproduced by allowed conventional parameter changes, the
measurement set is locally degenerate. Fewer parameters than observables is not enough.
Nonlinear cases may require full likelihood or parameter-space scans. Correlations must be
retained, and the same uncertainty must not be counted both as an explicit nuisance parameter
and as an independent covariance contribution.
These are different statistical questions. A universal three-to-five-sigma rule is not appropriate
for every comparison. Particle physics often uses a five-sigma discovery convention, whereas
exclusion and confidence intervals require their own calibrated procedures. Nuisance
parameters, boundaries, sensitivity, and searches over candidate signals matter [6]. A centralvalue rule such as “reject if a > 0.995” is insufficient.

The model is mathematically explicit, but its physical ontology remains a hypothesis. The rotor
and composite-Higgs sectors are not connected merely by sharing a name. A common
microscopic origin must supply a mapping of degrees of freedom, scales, and observables. No
complete Standard Model derivation, photon sector, or uniquely identifying FQCU observation
is established here.
The declared classical rotor kernel admits a local energy-continuity equation with an
antisymmetric bond current. A separate thermal Langevin model supplies an entropydissipation identity and a consistent nonequilibrium free-energy balance. Mutual information,
total correlation, phase order, and physical response describe different properties and must not
be conflated.
The compact quantum extension provides a defined operator theory and harmonic
benchmarks, not an automatic particle identification or a nonperturbative phase diagram.
Numerical refinements show why a single-run anomaly must not be promoted into a signature.
The observational coefficient matching illustrates how additional physical identifications can
already be constrained by existing data, while remaining conditional on those identifications.
The next decisive requirement is an additional physical rule leading to an identifiable
quantitative prediction and an independent test. The five-gate protocol makes that requirement
operational. This work therefore establishes a mathematical foundation and a disciplined
development plan, not experimental confirmation of FQCU.
